Abstract

Large language models reproduce the surface of a technical report within a few prompts, yet our own attempts to obtain a DeepSeek-style report from a project record failed in a consistent way: the prose was fluent, the section order was correct, and the numbers were invented. To further push the boundary of what a writing assistant can be trusted with, we present PaperPipe, a six-stage pipeline that fixes the order in which information must exist before prose is allowed, namely genre, evidence ledger, mined research points, skeleton, tool-rendered artifacts, and a deterministic gate check. On a draft produced through the pipeline we reach 12 on GateLint, the number of hard gates satisfied, and on the same checker a naive control draft written without any stage of the pipeline reaches 2 on GateLint, failing ten of twelve gates across the abstract, the contribution block, the navigation paragraph, the figure caption, and the failure declaration. Because the pipeline itself contains no trainable weights, we report 0 total parameters with 0 activated parameters per step, and what it adds instead is 161,739 bytes of protocol payload across 23 files; building the pipeline consumed 2.04M tokens and 0 GPU hours, since no stage performs gradient-based training. We also report a defect that our own checker misses: a comparison verb inserted without a supporting entry passes the gate that is supposed to catch it, because that gate counts admissible entries rather than verifying them. The released protocol and tools are available at https://github.com/zh2673-git/deepseek-paper.

1 Introduction

We wrote this paper with the very pipeline it describes, which makes the record unusually complete and the failure modes unusually visible. Our starting observation was not that models write badly; it was that models write plausibly and cite nothing. A control draft of 958 characters, produced by the most natural prompt we could write, opened with the three-part template that our gates exist to reject, namely a self-praising adjective, an unscoped superlative claim, and no number in the same sentence; it placed a related-work chapter as Section 2 and reported a score of 88.4 for a method that had never been run. Every defect in that draft was a defect of provenance rather than of style.

To turn the observations that define the DeepSeek report family into an executable protocol, we fix the sequence of six stages and forbid skipping, because each stage constrains the input of the next. Firstly, a ledger is built from the project directory before a sentence is written, and every entry is downgraded to todo until a person binds it to a claim. Secondly, research points are mined along five fixed interrogation lines, at most one main point per line, which converts an unbounded pile of observations into an explicit set of trade-offs. Furthermore, every figure and table is rendered from a structured description, so that a number has exactly one home. Lastly, twelve hard gates inspect the finished text for the markers that our corpus study found to be invariant across the twenty DeepSeek reports, and a separate ten-item rubric scores what the gates cannot see.

Our main contribution includes:

Evidence discipline: a ledger that precedes the prose

  • We design a two-stage ledger in which a scanner enumerates candidate sources and a person assigns the claim, the value, and the scope, which makes every admitted number traceable.
  • We make the writing stage read-only with respect to numbers, which removes the selection pressure that produced the invented scores in our control draft.

Mining budget: one research point per interrogation line

  • We propose five fixed interrogation lines, namely anomaly, trade-off, reuse, failure, and cost, which converts an unbounded pile of observations into an explicit set of trade-offs.
  • We impose a budget of one headline claim per line, which bounds how many points a draft may carry and forces the remainder into the discussion section.

Deterministic gates: style as a predicate

  • We introduce twelve gates G1–G12 that turn a measurable subset of "does it read like a DeepSeek report" into a pass/fail computation over plain text.
  • We report the cost of that transformation, namely that the gates encode necessary conditions only, rather than presenting them as a delivery criterion.

Tool-rendered artifacts: one home for every number

  • We design a structured description plus a renderer for tables and figures, which removes hand-copied cells and their drift against the text.
  • We measure what the elimination leaves uncovered, namely value verification rather than value binding.

1.1 Summary of Evaluation Results

On the mechanical axis, a draft produced through the full pipeline satisfies 12 on GateLint, and three of four fault-injected variants derived from it lose exactly 1 gate each, so a single injected fault is isolated rather than masked by an aggregate; the fourth variant, which inserts a comparison verb with no supporting entry, loses 0 gates, and we report it as a hole rather than repair it silently. On the statistical axis, the twelve regression cases reach 1.00 on EvalSuite over the eleven cases that carry a decidable signal, the twelfth being a judged case that stays pending until a judge command is supplied. The naive control draft satisfies 2 on GateLint and fails ten gates, and the two gates it satisfies are the only two that cannot fail on a short text, since it contains neither a figure nor a comparison verb.

In the remainder of this paper, we first present the pipeline as six stages and explain why the order is not negotiable (Section 2). We then describe the implementation, the tool protocol, and the payload budget (Section 3). Next, we report the gate behaviour, the fault-injection matrix, and the ablation of gate groups (Section 4). Thereafter, we discuss three failed attempts and the mechanism behind each of them (Section 5). Lastly, we conclude, state two limitations with their causes, and outline future directions (Section 6).

2 Approach

The pipeline is a fixed sequence of six stages. We organize this section by the order in which the stages were forced upon us by observed failures, rather than by module boundary, because each stage exists to close a hole that the previous stage left open.

2.1 Overview: the order is the mechanism

A stage may not be skipped, because each stage restricts the input of the next. Genre restricts the skeleton, the skeleton restricts which sections need evidence, the ledger restricts which numbers may appear at all, the mining budget restricts how many headline claims survive, and the gates reject any draft whose numbers cannot be traced. A pipeline that lets the writer choose the genre after the evidence is collected will systematically choose the genre for which the available evidence happens to be sufficient, which is exactly the behaviour we wanted to remove.

2.2 Evidence Ledger: making every number traceable

We design the ledger as a list of entries carrying an identifier, a kind, a claim, a value, a unit, a scope, a source, a status, and a list of the places where the entry is used. A scanner enumerates candidate files under the project root, classifies each as a measurement, a configuration, or an artifact, and attaches the first three lines of the file together with a flag recording whether the file contains digits. Every generated entry starts at todo, which means that no person has yet decided what the file is evidence for, and the writing stage may consume only entries whose status is ok.

The consequence is deliberately annoying. On this project the scanner produced 27 entries and all 27 were todo, so the first writing attempt had, in principle, no admissible number at all. We regard the annoyance as the mechanism, because the alternative, letting the writer read the directory and select numbers freely, is how the invented score in the control draft came to exist.

2.3 Five-Line Mining: one point per line

We propose to interrogate a project along exactly five lines: anomaly, meaning which observation contradicted expectation; trade-off, meaning what was given up and at what cost; reuse, meaning which component appears in more than one place; failure, meaning which attempt did not work and why; and cost, meaning what is cheaper than the conventional route and by how much. Each line may yield at most one main research point, and a point must be expressible as a section title of the form <mechanism>: <gain>. On this project the five lines produced the four mechanisms of Sections 2.2 to 2.5 plus one discarded candidate, and no line produced two headline points.

2.4 Deterministic Gates: turning style into a predicate

We introduce twelve gates over plain text in three groups. The provenance gates demand numbers to be present, to be paired, and to carry a unit (G1–G4). The structural gates demand a grouped contribution block, a navigation paragraph with cross-references, the absence of a standalone related-work chapter, and captions of at least two sentences (G5–G8). The honesty gates demand at least one failure declaration, a cost note, no forbidden phrase, no orphan figure, and a comparison verb backed by a traceable entry (G9–G12).

The trade-off is explicit and we state it rather than hide it: a gate is a regular expression or a structural scan, so it can be satisfied by a draft that is stylistically correct and substantively empty. Section 5.2 quantifies this gap and Section 6 explains why we keep the gates anyway.

2.5 Tool-Rendered Artifacts: one home for every number

We design the artifact path so that a table cell exists in exactly one place. The author writes a structured description holding the caption lead, the caption tail, the column list, the group column, the highlight policy, and the rows; a renderer emits booktabs LaTeX and Markdown and applies the best and second-best marking under a same-level tolerance. A figure description is handled the same way, and the renderer applies a three-line grid and a single accent colour. The cost is that no artifact can be produced before its data is structured, which front-loads work that a person would otherwise do by hand at the very end.

All four tools speak the same protocol: JSON on standard input under an args key, and JSON on standard output carrying a status flag and a result object. Note that the aforementioned costs include only the construction of the pipeline itself, excluding the cost of reading and excerpting the twenty source reports that the registry depends on.

4 Evaluation

4.1 Settings

We use two suites. GateLint runs the twelve gates over a draft and reports a per-gate verdict together with the evidence that produced it. EvalSuite holds twelve regression cases, each with an input and an expected behaviour, scored by three signal types, namely an assertion for mechanical checks, a rubric for scored quality, and a separate model call for judged cases. Eleven of the twelve cases carry a decidable signal, and the twelfth stays pending by design until a judge command is supplied.

Three kinds of draft are used: a compliant draft written through the full pipeline, a naive control draft written without any stage of the pipeline, and four fault-injected variants derived from the compliant draft, each carrying exactly one realistic defect. 4.3 What the numbers do not show

Three features of Table 2 deserve to be stated plainly rather than left to the reader. The first is that the two gates surviving in the naive control survive for a degenerate reason, so its count of 2 is not evidence that it satisfies anything. The second is that the four variants are not independent draws from a distribution of defects but four hand-chosen edits, so the table demonstrates detection on the cases we thought of and says nothing about the cases we did not. The third is the one we would rather not report: N4 reaches 12, the same count as the compliant draft, so a comparison verb with no supporting entry is not detected at all, and the honesty group that Table 3 credits with catching it is weaker than the group name suggests.

5 Discussion

5.1 Unsuccessful Attempts

Our first attempt was a style prompt, a list of nine hundred words of DeepSeek phrasing to imitate. It produced prose that a reader familiar with the corpus recognized as adjacent, and it produced invented numbers every time. The mechanism is straightforward: a phrasing list constrains surface form and leaves the mapping from claim to measurement unconstrained, so the path of least resistance is to keep the form and invent the content. A ledger does the opposite, constraining the mapping and leaving the surface free.

Our second attempt was one long checklist instead of twelve separate gates. We found that a checklist is read as advice rather than as a predicate: the same draft was scored differently on two passes, and no item was ever reported as a hard failure. Splitting it into independently computed gates removed the ambiguity, at the cost of losing the connective reasoning that a checklist provides in the one place where it is needed, namely judging whether a section earns its place.

Our third attempt was treating a green gate report as the delivery criterion. We built a draft satisfying all twelve gates and confirmed afterwards, by reading it, that it was still not deliverable: the numbers were traceable, the captions were long enough, and the argument was absent. The mechanism is that the twelve gates encode necessary conditions only. We now score every draft on ten qualitative items in addition to the gate report, and we keep a negative case in the regression suite whose only purpose is to remind us that a green report is not a verdict.

5.2 Dialogue: what the gates cannot see

A gate over plain text cannot distinguish a number that is correctly sourced from a number that is sourced to a document which does not contain it. We measured the size of this hole instead of asserting that it is small. Variant N4 inserts a comparison verb with no supporting entry and is scored with the ledger supplied. The checker builds its silence gate as a count comparison: it counts the comparison verbs in the text and counts the admissible comparison entries in the ledger, and passes the gate whenever the entry count reaches the verb count. Our ledger happens to carry one admissible comparison entry, so one unbacked verb satisfies the inequality, and N4 reaches 12, the same count as the compliant draft. We report this as a false negative of the mechanism rather than as a defect of the implementation, and the repair is structural: the gate has to bind each verb to a named entry instead of comparing two totals. Four qualitative dimensions remain out of reach of any count-based check, namely argument structure, whether a section earns its place, whether a cited source actually contains the asserted value, and whether the register matches the venue.

6 Conclusion, Limitations, and Future Directions

In this paper, we introduce PaperPipe, a gated pipeline that turns a project directory into a technical report in the style of the DeepSeek model reports. The pipeline fixes six stages in an order that makes provenance a precondition rather than an afterthought, mines at most one research point per interrogation line, renders every artifact from a structured description, and checks the finished text against twelve hard gates. A draft produced through the pipeline satisfies 12 on GateLint, while the naive control satisfies 2 on GateLint, and three of four injected faults are caught by exactly one gate each while the fourth is caught by none.

While acknowledging its usefulness, we also recognize that the pipeline has some limitations, especially on the boundary between necessary and sufficient conditions. Firstly, the gates encode necessary conditions only, so a compliant draft can still be substantively empty; we have observed this on our own output and now treat the gate report as a floor rather than a verdict. Secondly, the ledger cross-reference is a comparison of two totals rather than a binding of each assertion to a named entry, so a comparison verb may satisfy it while citing nothing, which is exactly what variant N4 demonstrates. Fortunately, these limitations are expected to be naturally addressed as long as the ecosystem provides machine-readable measurement records rather than prose summaries.

The pipeline adheres to the route of open protocols rather than of a monolithic tool, aiming to keep the evidence layer inspectable by a reader. In the future, we plan to strategically invest in research across the following directions.

  • We will extend the ledger from value binding to value verification, so that a gate can check whether the cited document actually contains the asserted number.
  • We will derive the gate set from the corpus rather than from our reading of it, so that a newly published report can update the gates without a manual pass.
  • We will add a judgement stage that scores the ten qualitative items with an independent model and records its disagreement with the author's own score.
  • We will keep a case in the regression suite for every gate that has ever been satisfied by a draft we would not deliver.

Appendix C Gate Definitions

The twelve gates are defined over plain text with regular expressions and structural scans, and each returns a verdict together with the evidence that produced it, namely the matched span, the count, or the offending line. A strict mode treats any failed gate as an overall failure, and a permissive mode returns the same verdicts without failing the run, which is used when the author wants the full list of problems in a single pass.
